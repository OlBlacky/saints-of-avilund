## Front Matter

### Cast

*Names are locked; the voice and the writing are Les's. Cues only below.*

- **Dr. Reinhardt Ostermann** — Praelector, St. Ignatius College, Lord's University, Waldheim. Sends the PCs. Suspects the commission's money is not the College's and chose not to trace it.
- **Prohor Zavetnik** — *Zapisnik* of the Order of the Candle, Library of Vyshgorod; Ostermann's correspondent at the sister-college. Engineered the errand, not the crisis.
- **Brother Ilya Bukvar** — the lone friar of the Shrine of St. Cyprian; the town's grammar school and scriptorium. A local boy schooled at Vyshgorod and returned in another canon's habit. Wrote the letter. The PCs' way in, and their reader of the Cyprian hand.
- **Father Zbignev Kostrev** — keeper of the Shrine of St. Carpathius, of the ruling House Kostrev. Controls the shrine and, through the family, the town. [[add text — Les: the Kostrev family and Zbignev's motivations]]

### The Season

*The adventure runs at harvest's end. It is cold, and it is about to get colder. Reference for the DM; the writing is Les's.*

**The two days.** The PCs arrive **30 October** — one day of daylight to work in. On **31 October** the **Feast of the Tithe** is held: everyone brings what they owe the Church and the town feasts together. The whole valley stands in one place, the flocks are down, every household is present, and Father Kostrev is at the height of his power as the shrine takes its tenth in kind. Then night falls on the 31st, which is a wicked night, when wicked things can happen — and will.

The date is black in Avilund and nobody in town needs to say why. Leave it ambient; no character recites the history.

**The clock:** the first frost shuts the high passes. Once they close, the PCs cannot leave the valley — and neither can anything else. Establish it in Chapter One.

**The table.** Millet porridge is the staple — thick, salted, eaten hot, then set and cut into slabs to carry to the fields. Flatbreads of barley, rye or millet on the hearthstones; wheat bread is a holy-day luxury. One root-stew pot never leaves the fire. For winter: cabbage soured in barrels with dill and horseradish (the brine drunk as well as eaten), sheep's-milk cheese packed hard into pine-bark cylinders, meat hung black in the chimney smoke, plum and apple paste boiled unsweetened in copper. Trout and hare fill the gaps. No vineyards this high — wine comes up the road dear, which the Voronovs can afford and the town cannot. Harvest indulgence is pressed apple and plum, honey and mead.

**The month's work.** The flocks come down and the town swells with shepherds. Millet threshed, orchards stripped, cabbage barrelled, old sheep culled and salted and smoked, winter rye sown, hay and firewood hauled. High-wheeled ox-carts; a wooden scratch-plough; iron only in the sickles and scythes, which makes the smith a man of consequence.

**The tithe.** Paid in kind at harvest — grain, cheese, cured meat — into the Shrine of St. Carpathius. This is the month Father Kostrev's authority is felt rather than believed. Poultry is forbidden on the holy days of the Saints.

**What the season is doing for you:** everyone is home, so no witness is off with the flocks. The shepherds have spent the summer closer to the Voronov house than anyone alive. Culling season means blood in the yards and smoke in every chimney, so the murders do not read as wrong at first glance. Every house has a full dark cellar. And the grammar school stands empty because every child is in the field — which is why Brother Bukvar had a month alone with his nerves in which to write the letter.
